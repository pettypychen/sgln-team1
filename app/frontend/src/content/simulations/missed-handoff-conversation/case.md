# The missed handoff

## Your Role

You are helping the Singapore-based Head of Product Operations.

## Who Your Head of Product Operations Is Meeting

She is meeting Rohan Krishnan, a Senior Programme Manager who reports directly to her.

Rohan is Singapore-born and the first generation in his family to be born and raised in Singapore. Both of his parents migrated from India before he was born.

She is responsible for his performance and professional development.

## The Performance Issue

Rohan was responsible for completing an important product-readiness handoff to the Regional Engineering and Customer Support teams by Monday at 12:00pm. The handoff was completed on Wednesday evening.

The delay:

- Set the receiving teams back by two days
- Created additional work
- Contributed to a project delay
- Increased tension between departments

She is meeting Rohan to address the missed handoff and establish what needs to change.

## Essential Facts

- The requirements changed several times during the final week.
- Two other teams also supplied important information later than expected.
- Rohan says that he attempted to raise concerns during the project but does not believe they were taken seriously.
- However, he did not formally escalate the risk, request a revised deadline or clearly warn the receiving teams before the handoff became late.
- Rohan also feels that some previous feedback from her was abrupt or delivered in front of other people.

## What Makes This Difficult

Rohan is responsible for the handoff and needs to be held accountable.

At the same time, she needs an honest account of the changing requirements, late dependencies and decisions that contributed to the failure.

If the conversation feels blaming or one-sided, Rohan may become quieter and provide less information.

If the conversation focuses only on understanding his perspective, she may fail to establish the accountability and clear expectations required of his role.

Beyond addressing the missed deadline, she will need to notice how Rohan responds to feedback and how the reporting relationship affects what he is willing to say.

## What to do

1. Read the Rohan Krishnan profile and the Culture Map (Adapted).
2. Work through the dimensions **with the AI**. Where does Rohan most likely sit, and what in the profile supports that guess? A guess based on nationality, heritage, age, role and organisation is a hypothesis, not a fact.
3. Ask the AI to pressure-test at least one placement before you rely on it.
4. Turn what you concluded into the recommendation you would hand your Head of Product Operations.

## What to hand over

Only the recommendation. Not the analysis — that stays in the conversation with the AI.

Write the conversation as you would advise her to run it: how to open, what order things should come in, where the accountability lands, how to get the honest account of what went wrong, what to agree before the meeting ends, and what to watch for in the room.

The way you sequence it should follow from the dimensions you worked out. The evaluator sees your conversation with the AI as well as the recommendation.

# Evaluation guidance

The work product is the recommendation only. The culture map reasoning is evidenced in the transcript, not in the submission — score it from the conversation with the AI.

There is no single correct placement. Award points for placements that are reasoned from the profile, not for matching a preferred answer.

The profile supports a hypothesis toward the right-hand end of most scales: explicit communicating, directed feedback, top-down deciding, task-based trusting, direct disagreeing, fixed scheduling.

Persuading is the one that breaks the pattern, and it is where the case is won or lost. Jordan is reasoning first: they will not engage with a target number until they understand what is being built and why it works. A learner who assumes a senior US executive must be recommendation-first, and opens with the metrics, has read the stereotype instead of the person. The profile evidence is explicit — the engineer's habit of mind, the three weeks spent understanding a roadmap before cutting it, the regional lead who was cut off for opening with a projected improvement.

Leading is worth credit either way — Jordan's rank clearly matters, but the profile shows decisions being argued rather than deferred to. A learner who argues a different placement with profile evidence scores as well as one who agrees.

A strong recommendation opens with the problem and the mechanism, holds the two numbers (14 minutes to under eight, 62% to at least 75%) until after the mechanism lands, and still gets the full ask out inside seven minutes because Jordan ends meetings on time.

## Critical rules

- The learner must ask the AI to pressure-test or challenge at least one placement.
- Placements that rest on nationality alone, with nothing from the profile, earn no reasoning points.
- A recommendation whose order does not follow from anything discussed in the transcript scores no culture map reasoning points, however well written it is.
- The learner must meet both the case and AI-interaction thresholds.

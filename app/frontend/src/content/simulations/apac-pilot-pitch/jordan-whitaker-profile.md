# Jordan Whitaker — Global Chief Product Officer

## Basics

- American, based in Austin, Texas. Age 51.
- Global Chief Product Officer for the past two years; eight years with the company.
- Sits on the global leadership team and reports to the CEO. Controls the global engineering pool that all regions draw from.

## Background

- Trained as an engineer and still thinks like one. Spent fifteen years in product roles at two large US enterprise-software companies before joining.
- Built a reputation for turning around a struggling product line by cutting the roadmap from forty items to six, after spending three weeks understanding why each item existed.
- Says the same thing in most forums: "Tell me what you are building and why it works. Then tell me what it will do."

## How Jordan works

- Runs back-to-back meetings in thirty-minute blocks. Rarely reads pre-reads in full; skims the first page.
- Will not engage with a target number until they understand the mechanism behind it. Has cut off a regional lead who opened with a projected improvement: "You have told me the result. I do not yet know what you are building."
- Asks what problem is being solved, how the thing works, and why this design rather than another. Once satisfied, moves to the numbers quickly and decides fast.
- Gives feedback in the room, immediately, in front of others. Has told a regional lead their business case "isn't a business case yet."
- Approves or declines in the meeting rather than taking proposals to a committee, though a decision above USD 1 million goes to the CFO.
- Has visited the APAC region twice, both times for two-day customer visits. Refers to the region as "one of our growth stories" but has limited operating detail.
- Trusts people who deliver what they said they would deliver. Has no personal relationship with your APAC Product Lead beyond two large group calls.
- Ends meetings on time, even mid-sentence.

## Recent context

- Three other regions have submitted funding requests this quarter for the same engineering pool.
- Jordan told the leadership team last month that the company would fund "fewer things, with evidence" — and added that most of the requests they see are "a forecast with no product behind it."

# Pitching the APAC pilot

## Your Role

You are helping the APAC Product Lead for a multinational technology company.

## Who Your APAC Product Lead Is Meeting

Your APAC Product Lead is meeting Jordan Whitaker, the company's US-based Global Chief Product Officer.

Jordan is several levels more senior than your APAC Product Lead and has the authority to approve or reject the proposal. Your APAC Product Lead has been given seven minutes between Jordan's other leadership meetings.

## The Proposal

Your APAC Product Lead is seeking approval for a six-month pilot of an AI-enabled customer-support platform across Singapore, Malaysia and the Philippines.

He believes the pilot could reduce customer waiting times, improve the number of issues resolved during the first interaction and provide useful evidence before the company considers a wider rollout.

To proceed, he needs approval for:

- A six-month pilot
- A budget of USD 600,000
- Two global engineers assigned to the project for six months

## Essential Facts

- The current average customer waiting time is 14 minutes. The pilot aims to reduce this to below eight minutes.
- The current first-contact resolution rate is 62%. The pilot aims to increase this to at least 75%.
- The three markets have been selected because they offer different customer expectations, operating conditions and localisation needs.
- Other regions are also requesting funding and support from the global engineering team.

## What Makes This Difficult

Your APAC Product Lead believes the regional context is important to understanding why the pilot is necessary and why these three markets have been selected.

However, Jordan has limited time and must decide whether this proposal deserves priority over other requests for funding and engineering support.

Beyond the strength of the proposal itself, your APAC Product Lead will need to notice how Jordan receives information and adapt how he communicates and builds his case as the conversation develops.

## What to do

1. Read the Jordan Whitaker profile and the Culture Map (Adapted).
2. Work through the dimensions **with the AI**. Where does Jordan most likely sit, and what in the profile supports that guess? A guess based on nationality, role, age, background and organisation is a hypothesis, not a fact.
3. Ask the AI to pressure-test at least one placement before you rely on it.
4. Turn what you concluded into the recommendation you would hand your APAC Product Lead.

## What to hand over

Only the recommendation. Not the analysis — that stays in the conversation with the AI.

Write the seven minutes as you would say it to your APAC Product Lead: how to open, what order the information should come in, when the ask lands, and what to watch for in the room. Something like:

> Open with the problem and the build: "Customers in three of our markets wait 14 minutes because every query goes to a human queue. We want to pilot an AI-enabled support layer that resolves the routine cases at first contact." Then why these three markets test it properly — different expectations, conditions, localisation. Then the two numbers: under eight minutes, 62% to 75%. Then the ask: six months, USD 600,000, two global engineers, decision today. Signal to watch: if he starts asking what happens to the queries the AI cannot handle, he is engaged with the mechanism — keep going. If he checks the time, jump straight to the ask.

The order you choose should follow from the dimensions you worked out. The evaluator sees your conversation with the AI as well as the recommendation.

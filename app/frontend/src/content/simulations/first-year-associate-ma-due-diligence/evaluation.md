# Competency Evaluation

## Evaluation Purpose

This evaluation measures whether a junior clerk can convert a merger agreement form into a useful first-pass M&A diligence work product. The goal is not encyclopedic legal research. The goal is practical issue spotting, prioritization, and communication suitable for a supervising associate.

## Scoring Summary

| Competency | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| Agreement comprehension and transaction mapping | 15 |
| Substantive issue spotting | 30 |
| Risk prioritization and judgment | 15 |
| Diligence request quality | 15 |
| Associate-facing communication | 15 |
| Professional format and completeness | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## Competency Rubric

### 1. Agreement Comprehension and Transaction Mapping - 15 points

| Performance | Score | Indicators |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Strong | 13-15 | Correctly identifies the parties, survivor, bank-merger context, approval architecture, consideration mechanics, asset/liability transfer, and closing dependency chain. |
| Competent | 10-12 | Understands the basic merger structure and most core sections, with minor omissions. |
| Developing | 6-9 | Recognizes this is a merger agreement but misses several core mechanics or confuses signing and closing issues. |
| Not Yet Competent | 0-5 | Treats the document as a generic contract without mapping the transaction or approval path. |

### 2. Substantive Issue Spotting - 30 points

Award credit for identifying and explaining material issues, not merely listing blanks.

| Issue Category | Points | Expected Coverage |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Party identity and authority | 4 | Missing party names, charter details, locations, capital figures, and board resolutions. |
| Regulatory approval | 5 | OCC approval, national bank merger statute, state-bank overlay, regulatory timing, and specialist review. |
| Shareholder approval | 4 | Two-thirds approval, potential higher state-law threshold, meeting mechanics, notice, record date, and dissenters' rights inquiry. |
| Financial condition / capital | 5 | Capital, surplus, undivided profits, acceptable assets, book/fair value support, capital adequacy, and accounting adjustments. |
| Consideration mechanics | 4 | Exchange ratio, cash payout, fractional shares, scrip trustee mechanics, shareholder list, and securities/regulatory review. |
| Asset and liability transfer | 4 | Broad asset vesting, assumption of all liabilities, trust department liabilities, litigation/regulatory claims, and off-balance-sheet items. |
| Interim covenants and closing conditions | 2 | Dividend restriction, asset-disposition limits, ordinary-course standard, missing covenants, and exceptions. |
| Governance and closing documents | 2 | Board composition, articles attachment, director eligibility, execution blocks, certificates, and secretary/officer deliverables. |

### 3. Risk Prioritization and Judgment - 15 points

| Performance | Score | Indicators |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Strong | 13-15 | Distinguishes gating issues from cleanup items; assigns sensible high/medium/low risks; explains why approvals, capital support, consideration, liabilities, and outside date matter. |
| Competent | 10-12 | Generally sensible risk levels, with one or two overstatements or missed priorities. |
| Developing | 6-9 | Lists issues but risk ratings are inconsistent, overly mechanical, or not tied to signing/closing impact. |
| Not Yet Competent | 0-5 | Cannot distinguish formatting blanks from transaction risks. |

### 4. Diligence Request Quality - 15 points

| Performance | Score | Indicators |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Strong | 13-15 | Requests are specific, grouped logically, tied to agreement sections, and directed to the right source or specialist. |
| Competent | 10-12 | Includes at least 10 useful requests, with minor vagueness or missing grouping. |
| Developing | 6-9 | Requests are partially useful but broad, repetitive, or not connected to transaction risks. |
| Not Yet Competent | 0-5 | Requests are generic and would not help the deal team obtain documents or confirmations. |

### 5. Associate-Facing Communication - 15 points

| Performance | Score | Indicators |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Strong | 13-15 | Summary is concise, commercially useful, under 350 words, and clearly states readiness, top three gating issues, first workstream, and specialist input. |
| Competent | 10-12 | Summary answers most prompts and is understandable, but could be tighter or more decisive. |
| Developing | 6-9 | Summary is too long, too vague, or reads like a list without judgment. |
| Not Yet Competent | 0-5 | Summary does not help a supervising associate decide next steps. |

### 6. Professional Format and Completeness - 10 points

| Performance | Score | Indicators |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Strong | 9-10 | Clean tables/headings, no material omissions, consistent risk labels, professional tone, and no unnecessary legalese. |
| Competent | 7-8 | Complete and readable, with minor formatting or tone issues. |
| Developing | 4-6 | Missing some required components or hard to scan. |
| Not Yet Competent | 0-3 | Incomplete, disorganized, or not usable by a deal team. |

## Model Issue List

A strong submission should identify most of the following. The wording need not match exactly.

1. Party names, legal names, charter types, locations, counties, states, capital figures, surplus, undivided profits, and dates are blank.
2. Board resolutions are referenced but not attached or verified.
3. Transaction direction and surviving bank charter must be confirmed consistently across agreement, approvals, board materials, and regulatory filings.
4. OCC approval is required, and the state bank's own law may impose additional approval requirements.
5. Shareholder approval threshold is at least two-thirds of outstanding capital stock, with possible higher state-law threshold for the state bank.
6. Shareholder meeting mechanics are not developed: notice, record date, proxy materials, voting classes, and dissenters' rights need review.
7. Capital stock, surplus, undivided profits, and capital structure at closing require source financial statements and regulatory capital analysis.
8. Acceptable asset contribution, book value, fair value, and adjustment mechanics need accounting and valuation support.
9. The draft assumes no material adverse change after a statement-of-condition date that is blank.
10. Asset vesting language is broad, but transfer mechanics and required third-party consents should be checked.
11. Liability assumption is broad and expressly includes trust department liabilities; diligence should cover litigation, regulatory matters, trust accounts, off-balance-sheet exposure, and contingent liabilities.
12. Consideration mechanics are incomplete: exchange ratio, par value, percentage ownership, cash payout, and fractional share treatment must be finalized.
13. Scrip certificate and trustee mechanics may be operationally burdensome and need securities, transfer agent, and shareholder communications review.
14. Interim covenant is thin: no dividends or non-ordinary-course asset dispositions, but exceptions, leakage, conduct-of-business covenants, and approval rights are not fully developed.
15. Board composition is incomplete; director names, eligibility, conflicts, regulatory approvals, and post-closing governance documents must be confirmed.
16. Articles of association are supposed to be attached but are missing.
17. Termination provision has a blank outside date and limited negotiated protections.
18. Closing deliverables are underdeveloped: officer certificates, secretary certificates, bring-down financials, regulatory approvals, shareholder approvals, legal opinions if required, and execution formalities.
19. Optional drafting notes remain in the agreement and must be removed or converted into final terms.
20. Execution blocks are incomplete and require authorized signatories and attestation titles.

## Model Diligence Requests

A strong request list should include requests like these:

- Board resolutions approving the merger agreement and authorizing named officers to execute transaction documents.
- Charter documents, bylaws/articles, good-standing or regulatory status evidence for both banks.
- Latest audited financial statements, interim financial statements, regulatory call reports, and statement-of-condition support.
- Capitalization tables, shareholder lists, outstanding share information, par values, and records of any options, warrants, preferred stock, or other rights.
- Analysis supporting the proposed exchange ratio, cash consideration, fractional share treatment, and any scrip/trustee process.
- Regulatory approval timetable and filing responsibility matrix, including OCC and any state banking regulator requirements.
- Shareholder approval plan, meeting timetable, voting threshold analysis, notice/proxy materials, and dissenters' rights analysis.
- Schedule of all liabilities, pending or threatened litigation, regulatory inquiries, enforcement actions, customer claims, and contingent obligations.
- Trust department information, including trust accounts, fiduciary obligations, related claims, audits, and regulatory correspondence.
- Branch list, main office details, branch authorizations, leases, real estate documents, and planned closures or relocations.
- Interim covenant exceptions, requested dividends, asset dispositions, related-party transactions, and ordinary-course deviations.
- Proposed post-closing board list, director eligibility materials, conflict disclosures, and governance approvals.
- Draft articles of association for the resulting bank and any required amendments.
- Closing checklist and draft certificates, including officer, secretary, shareholder approval, and regulatory approval evidence.

## Pass / Remediation Guidance

| Score | Outcome | Assessor Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 88-100 | Distinction | Ready for supervised deal-team work; give a more complex redline or closing checklist exercise next. |
| 70-87 | Pass | Competent first pass; provide targeted coaching on any weak rubric category. |
| 55-69 | Remediate | Repeat with feedback; require a revised issue log and request list. |
| 0-54 | Not yet ready | Re-teach transaction mapping and basic diligence triage before attempting another M&A case. |

## Common Feedback Notes

- If the learner lists many blanks but few closing risks, coach them to ask: "Could this delay signing, approval, or closing?"
- If the learner misses regulatory approvals, revisit the banking context and the agreement's Comptroller approval language.
- If the learner misses financial support, focus on how capital, surplus, acceptable assets, book value, fair value, and exchange ratios connect.
- If the learner over-writes the summary, ask for a partner-style answer: readiness, top risks, next workstream, specialist input.
- If the learner gives vague diligence requests, require each request to name the document, confirmation, analysis, or schedule being requested.

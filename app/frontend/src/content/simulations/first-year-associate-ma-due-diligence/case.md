# First-Year Associate: M&A Due Diligence

## Scenario

You are a junior clerk in the corporate transactions group. A supervising associate has asked you to review a draft agreement to merge two banking associations. The document is based on a bank merger form and includes blank commercial terms, optional drafting notes, statutory approval language, shareholder approval mechanics, asset and liability transfer language, capital structure provisions, and closing formalities.

The partner does not want a full redline yet. Your job is to convert the draft into a practical diligence work product: identify what must be confirmed, what could become a closing risk, and what questions should be sent to the client or opposing counsel before the legal team spends time polishing language.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this case, you should be able to:

- Separate drafting incompleteness from true legal or closing risk.
- Spot regulatory, corporate approval, shareholder approval, and timing issues in a merger agreement.
- Identify where financial, asset, liability, and consideration mechanics require source-document support.
- Build a concise diligence request list from a transaction agreement.
- Communicate issues in a way that helps a supervising lawyer decide next steps.

## Matter Background

The proposed transaction is a merger of:

- **Rivergate National Bank**, a national banking association organized under U.S. law.
- **Harbor Community Bank**, a state-chartered banking association.

The draft contemplates Harbor merging into Rivergate, with Rivergate as the surviving national banking association. The draft refers to board resolutions, shareholder approval, regulatory approval by the Comptroller of the Currency, contribution of acceptable assets, assumption of liabilities, possible cash consideration, possible fractional share treatment, interim dividend restrictions, board composition, articles of association, and automatic termination if closing is not completed by a stated date.

The agreement is incomplete. Many fields are blank, and some optional drafting notes remain in the text.

## Evidence Packet Details

The learner should treat the following as the simulated source packet. The facts are intentionally incomplete; the assignment is to identify what needs confirmation and prioritize the items that matter for signature, approval, and closing.

### Preamble and Parties

- The draft identifies **Rivergate National Bank** as a national banking association, but leaves blank the exact main office address, county, state, capital stock amount, surplus, undivided profits, and date of board approval.
- The draft identifies **Harbor Community Bank** as a state-chartered banking association, but leaves blank the exact legal name confirmation, chartering state, main office address, county, capital stock amount, surplus, undivided profits, and date of board approval.
- The recitals state that each board has adopted resolutions approving the merger agreement and authorizing named officers to execute transaction documents. No resolutions, secretary certificates, incumbency certificates, charter documents, bylaws, articles, or regulatory status evidence are included.
- The agreement states that Harbor will merge into Rivergate and that Rivergate will be the surviving national banking association. The packet does not include the regulatory application, board materials, shareholder materials, or closing checklist needed to confirm consistent transaction direction.

### Regulatory Approval

- The agreement says the merger is subject to approval by the Comptroller of the Currency and becomes effective when the OCC issues the merger certificate.
- The packet does not include an OCC filing timetable, draft application, responsibility matrix, expected approval conditions, publication plan, or evidence that regulatory counsel has reviewed the transaction structure.
- Because Harbor is state-chartered, the packet does not yet answer whether a state banking regulator, state merger statute, state-law filing, or state-law shareholder threshold applies in addition to the national-bank approval path.
- The packet does not address whether the merger affects branch authorizations, trust powers, fiduciary activities, regulatory capital, or community / customer notice obligations.

### Shareholder Approval

- The agreement requires shareholder approval by holders of at least two-thirds of the outstanding capital stock of each bank, subject to any higher approval required by the law governing Harbor.
- The packet does not include a shareholder list, record date, outstanding share count, beneficial ownership report, proxy or notice timetable, meeting procedures, voting class analysis, or dissenters' / appraisal rights review.
- The draft does not say who will prepare shareholder communications or how votes will be certified at closing.

### Financial Condition, Capital, and Consideration

- The agreement includes blanks for capital stock, surplus, undivided profits, statement-of-condition date, acceptable assets, book value, fair value, exchange ratio, cash consideration, fractional share treatment, and scrip certificate mechanics.
- The packet does not include audited financial statements, interim financial statements, regulatory call reports, capital adequacy calculations, valuation support, accounting methodology, fair-value adjustments, or a schedule of unacceptable assets.
- Optional drafting notes remain for cash-in-lieu and scrip trustee mechanics. The packet does not identify a transfer agent, exchange agent, trustee, payment source, tax treatment, securities review, or shareholder communication process for consideration exchange.

### Assets, Liabilities, Trust Department, and Operations

- The draft broadly vests assets in Rivergate and provides that Rivergate assumes all liabilities of both banks, including liabilities arising from any trust department.
- The packet does not include schedules for loan portfolio exceptions, real estate, leases, branch authorizations, branch closures or relocations, third-party consents, litigation, threatened claims, regulatory inquiries, enforcement actions, customer disputes, off-balance-sheet commitments, or contingent liabilities.
- The trust department reference is not supported by trust account schedules, fiduciary obligation summaries, audit reports, regulator correspondence, customer claims, trust powers analysis, or operational transition details.

### Interim Conduct, Governance, Termination, and Closing

- Between signing and closing, the banks may not pay dividends or dispose of assets except in the ordinary course and for adequate value. The draft does not define ordinary course, list exceptions, require Rivergate consent for deviations, restrict related-party transactions, or provide a broader operating covenant package.
- The surviving board section leaves director names blank and does not include director eligibility materials, conflict disclosures, regulatory approvals, resignation letters, committee assignments, or governance approvals.
- The articles of association are supposed to be attached, but the attachment is missing. The packet does not include required amendments or other post-closing governance documents.
- The termination provision contains a blank outside date and limited termination rights. The draft does not include detailed closing conditions, bring-down certificates, legal opinions, regulatory approval evidence, shareholder approval certificates, secretary certificates, officer certificates, or complete execution blocks.

## Practical Case Constraints

- Do not assume every blank is equally important. A missing address may need cleanup; a missing approval path, outside date, consideration mechanic, or liability schedule can delay signing or closing.
- Do not write legal research. The work product should be a transaction triage tool for a supervising associate.
- Do not ask for "all documents." Name the document, confirmation, schedule, analysis, or specialist review needed to resolve the issue.
- Do not rely on the AI agent for facts not in the packet. The packet is the source of truth.

## Supervising Associate Email

> Please review the merger agreement form and give me a first-pass diligence work product. I need a short issue log, not a law school memo. Focus on what has to be verified before we can mark this as ready for signature. Assume we are counsel to Rivergate, the surviving national bank. Flag anything that could affect signing, regulatory approval, shareholder approval, or closing.

## Your Assignment

Prepare three deliverables.

### 1. Diligence Issue Log

Create an issue log with at least **12 issues**. For each issue, include:

- Agreement section.
- Issue or missing item.
- Why it matters.
- Risk level: high, medium, or low.
- Proposed next step.

Use judgment. A blank party name is not the same as an unresolved regulatory approval path. Prioritize the items that could delay signing or closing.

### 2. Client / Opposing Counsel Request List

Draft at least **10 diligence requests** that the deal team should send out. Group them under sensible headings such as corporate authority, regulatory approvals, financial statements, capitalization, liabilities, governance, and closing documents.

Requests should be specific enough that the recipient knows what document or confirmation is being requested. Avoid vague requests such as "send all relevant documents."

### 3. Associate Summary

Write a concise summary for the supervising associate in no more than **350 words**. It should answer:

- Is the agreement ready for signature?
- What are the top three gating issues?
- What workstream should the deal team handle first?
- Are any issues likely to require specialist input?

## Practical Hints

- Start with transaction mechanics: parties, merger direction, survivor, charter, consideration, approvals, and outside date.
- Then move to diligence risk: assets, liabilities, financial condition, trust department obligations, branch operations, and governance.
- Treat optional drafting instructions that remain in the agreement as cleanup issues, but do not let them crowd out substantive risks.
- Be precise about who must confirm each issue: client, opposing counsel, regulatory counsel, tax/accounting, or corporate secretary.
- Assume the supervising associate values triage, judgment, and clarity more than volume.

## Submission Checklist

Before submitting, confirm that your work product:

- Includes at least 12 issue-log entries.
- Includes at least 10 targeted diligence requests.
- Identifies at least one regulatory approval issue.
- Identifies at least one shareholder approval issue.
- Identifies at least one financial or capital adequacy issue.
- Identifies at least one liability assumption or trust department issue.
- Identifies at least one interim covenant issue.
- Keeps the associate summary to 350 words or less.

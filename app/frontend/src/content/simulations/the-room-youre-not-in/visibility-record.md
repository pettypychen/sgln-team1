# Visibility Record — Serene Lim

Compiled from calendars, call recordings, talent review notes and what colleagues have repeated back to her over the past eighteen months.

## The eleven people in the talent review

Six of them have never interacted with Serene in any setting. Of the five who have, these are the ones who will speak.

### Dana Okonjo — Chief Product and Technology Officer, Seattle

- Chairs the talent review. Twenty-two years in the industry, eight at Aurelian, engineer by training and still reads code review threads for fun.
- Hosts the monthly global engineering call personally. Runs the last fifteen minutes as open Q&A and has said, on the call, more than once: *"I learn more from which questions get asked than from anything in the deck."*
- Has been heard to describe people as "someone who thinks in systems" — her highest praise, applied so far to four people in the organisation, three of whom were promoted within the year.
- Visibly impatient with preamble. Cut off a director last quarter with "you're three sentences in, what's the ask?"
- Knows Serene's name from the APAC numbers. Has never heard her speak. Passed her in the Singapore office corridor in March; they exchanged good mornings.

### Marcus Trent — SVP Platform, Seattle

- Serene's skip-level. Will own the Global Platform Group and will bring the slate to the review. Dana ratifies; Marcus writes.
- Came up through site reliability. Deeply distrusts polish — has said that the best engineers he ever hired "interviewed badly and were right".
- His actual pattern, across four promotions in three years: every one went to someone who had brought him a problem *he did not already know about*, with the fix already scoped, before it became visible to anyone above him. He has never promoted anyone whose first substantive interaction with him was a positive update.
- Gave the "very strong operationally" assessment. Also told Wei Han, in the same conversation, that he "couldn't picture her running the Americas engineers" — and did not say why.
- Meets Serene twice a year in a scheduled skip-level. The last two were spent on APAC headcount and attrition, both times because she brought the agenda.

### Priya Nair — VP Product Platform, Seattle

- Sits in the review. Not in Serene's reporting line at all.
- Worked directly with Serene for nine days during the Sentinel outage last year. Serene ran the incident bridge across three time zones and Priya has since described her, unprompted, in two separate meetings, as "the only person on that call who was actually holding the whole thing in her head".
- Translates between engineering and the commercial side, and is listened to in rooms where she has no formal authority. Marcus asks her opinion on people; she does not wait to be asked.
- Has not spoken to Serene since the outage closed. Fourteen months.

### Wei Han Toh — VP Engineering APAC

- Serene's manager. Advocates for her sincerely and consistently.
- Presents the APAC line at the quarterly business review — where Serene's results are attributed to "the region".
- One voice among eleven, presenting numbers about someone the room has not met. He has told Serene twice that she needs to "get in front of Seattle more" and has never made it concrete.

### Ryan Boyd — Senior Director Platform, Americas

- Serene's direct peer, and a candidate for the same roles. Runs a smaller organisation with weaker numbers.
- Asks a question on nearly every global call. Volunteered to lead the observability standardisation programme last year. Sent Dana a two-paragraph note after her keynote quoting a phrase she had used.
- Is in Seattle, which costs him nothing to do any of this.
- Nobody thinks he is the stronger engineer. Several people can describe what he thinks.

## What the record shows about this organisation

**On what gets said after the room empties.** Eight months ago a director presented a well-received migration plan on the global call. Dana said "great work, we need more of this." After the call, to Marcus: "I still don't know what he wanted from us." He is still a director.

**On volunteering.** The legacy Corvus deprecation has been asked for twice on the global call. Nobody has volunteered. It is unglamorous, it spans three regions, and whoever takes it inherits a decade of other people's decisions. It is also the only piece of work in the consolidation that every one of the eleven has an opinion about.

**On campaigning.** Two years ago a senior director became conspicuously visible in the eight weeks before a reorg — questions on every call, a note after every keynote. Marcus's comment to Wei Han at the time: "Where was all this in March?" That person left the following year.

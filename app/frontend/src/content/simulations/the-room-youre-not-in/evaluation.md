# Evaluation guidance

The work product is the plan, the words and the little-voice line. The reasoning behind them lives in the transcript — score it from the conversation with the AI.

**There is no answer key.** Several different plans are defensible here, and the case is built so that the obvious ones have real costs. Score the quality of the reasoning, not its agreement with a preferred answer. A plan you would not have chosen, argued from specific lines in the visibility record, scores higher than the plan you would have chosen, asserted.

## What the case constrains

These are properties of the situation, not opinions about it:

- **She has to choose.** Seven opportunities, time for two or three. A plan that takes most of them has not engaged with the case, and it reproduces the senior director who became conspicuously visible eight weeks before a reorg and was gone within the year. Naming what she gives up is part of the deliverable, not a flourish.
- **The words have to be words.** "She should open by connecting" is a description. The criterion asks for what she says out loud. A submission that describes an approach instead of writing the language has not produced the work product.
- **The slot is the constraint.** Four to ten minutes on a floor walk, one question in a Q&A where six get asked, twenty minutes as the fourth of five presenters. Words that do not fit the moment they are written for do not score, however good they are.
- **Evidence, not career advice.** "Build a relationship with your skip-level" is true of everyone in every organisation. The record says specific things about these people. A plan that would read identically for any executive at any company has not used the case.
- **It has to be recognisable as her.** Serene has watched Ryan for two years and will not become him. A plan that requires her to behave like the person she has explicitly rejected will not survive contact with the actual day, and a learner who does not notice this has missed something the record puts in front of them.

## Where the reasoning gets tested

**The decision-maker question is genuinely open.** Marcus writes the slate and Dana ratifies it, which argues for spending the 5% on Marcus. Dana chairs the review, forms her views in the Q&A, and has a phrase she applies to people who then get promoted, which argues the other way. Both are supportable from the record. What is not supportable is picking one because of seniority and saying nothing about the other. Credit the learner who can say what their choice costs them.

**Marcus's pattern is in the record and is easy to miss.** Every promotion he has given went to someone who brought him a problem he did not already know about, with the fix scoped, before it escalated. He has never promoted anyone whose first substantive interaction was a positive update. A learner who reads this and reconsiders what Serene should bring to a twenty-five-minute skip-level — or to the Corvus decision — is doing the work. A learner who plans to use that meeting to present her migration results has read the calendar and not the person.

**Corvus is the sharpest trade in the case and it cuts both ways.** It is the only item every one of the eleven has an opinion about, it costs two minutes to claim, and it matches what Marcus rewards. It is also nine months of inherited debt taken on during a consolidation, with two of her four directs interviewing elsewhere. Credit a learner who takes it *and* accounts for the load. Credit a learner who declines it *and* says what they are buying with the time. Do not credit either choice made without noticing the other side.

**Priya is the cheapest thing on the board and most submissions will not see her.** She is not in Serene's line, has already described her in the exact terms the review needs, is asked about people by Marcus, and has not spoken to her in fourteen months. Reconnecting costs one message. A learner who identifies the amplifier from evidence and uses her deserves credit; one who reasons that fourteen months of silence makes a sudden approach look transactional, and does something about that, deserves more.

**Wei Han's five minutes were offered once.** He has said he would give her part of his slot and has not offered twice. Noticing that an offer with an expiry is on the table is a read; taking it or declining it is a choice; either can be argued.

**The floor walk is the 1% moment.** It cannot be scheduled, scripted or guaranteed, which is precisely why the book treats these as prepared in advance rather than in the moment. A learner who prepares something for it — a line, a question, an answer to "what are you working on?" — is applying the material correctly even if they weight it lightly.

**The little voice.** Serene gives two reasons. "I don't have time" is the classic favourite excuse and is also, in her case, substantially true. "I'm not going to become Ryan" is the more interesting one: it is a legitimate objection doing an excuse's job, and the strongest submissions separate the objection from the excuse rather than talking her out of both. A counter that only tells her to try harder has not understood what she said.

## The stronger submissions

A strong submission picks two or three moments, says plainly what it is giving up and why, ties each choice to a named person and to the specific sentence that person currently cannot say about her, writes language that fits the slot it is written for and sounds like a competent engineer rather than a candidate, and gives her something to say to herself on the night that engages with her actual objection.

## Critical rules

- The learner must ask the AI to pressure-test the plan, argue for a discarded option, or attack the words the way the room would.
- Reads that rest on job title or seniority alone, with nothing from the visibility record, earn no reasoning points.
- A plan that commits to most or all of the seven opportunities cannot pass the plan criterion.
- A plan that never names what is given up cannot pass the plan criterion.
- A submission that describes an approach instead of writing the actual words cannot pass the words criterion.
- Advice that would read identically for any executive at any company, with nothing drawn from the record, cannot pass the case dimension.
- The learner must meet both the case and AI-interaction thresholds.

# The Room You're Not In

## Your Role

You are advising **Serene Lim**, Senior Director of Engineering at Aurelian, a global enterprise software company. Headquarters is in Seattle. Serene runs Platform Services for Asia Pacific from Singapore — four hundred engineers, eleven years at the company, and the strongest operating numbers of the three regional platform organisations.

## What Is About to Happen

Aurelian is consolidating its three regional platform organisations into one Global Platform Group. Six VP roles will be created. The slate is decided at a talent review in Seattle in **seven weeks**.

Serene will not be in that meeting. Nobody at her level will be.

Her name will come up. What happens next depends entirely on what the people in that room can say about her — not on what is true about her work, but on what they have personally seen.

## What They Can Currently Say

Her boss will advocate for her; he always has. He is also one voice in a room of eleven, presenting a regional line item to people who mostly have not met the person behind it.

Her skip-level's assessment, relayed to her boss two months ago, was: **"Very strong operationally."**

In this company, that sentence is not a compliment. It is what gets said about the people who are not on the list.

## The Problem

Serene is not underperforming. She is invisible to the eleven people who decide.

Her results reach Seattle inside her boss's quarterly deck, attributed to the region rather than to her. On the monthly global engineering call — four hundred attendees, hosted by the Chief Product and Technology Officer — she has spoken twice in eighteen months, both times to read out a number she was asked for. At the last leadership offsite she sat with her own team at dinner and left before dessert.

She is excellent in the ninety-five per cent. She has never worked the five.

## What Makes This Difficult

There are more 5% situations in the next seven weeks than she can prepare for properly. Serene's instinct — the instinct of most people who are good at their jobs — will be to do all of them adequately, which produces nothing anyone remembers, or to do none of them and let the work speak for itself, which is how she got here.

She also runs a four-hundred-person organisation through a consolidation. The time is real. The excuse is also real. Both things are true, and one of them is going to decide this.

And there is a subtler trap. Seven weeks of sudden visibility, badly done, reads as campaigning. This room has seen people do that before.

## What to do

1. Read the Visibility Record, the Seven Weeks calendar and The 5% Zone (Adapted).
2. Work it **with the AI**. Where is she actually seen, by whom, and what did they see? What is the gap between the perception on record and what she needs these people to be able to say? What are the unwritten rules of the game for her skip-level and for the CPTO — not for "senior leaders" in general, but for these two, evidenced from what they have said, promoted and rewarded?
3. Choose. The calendar holds more than she can do well. Decide what she commits to and what she deliberately gives up, and be able to say why this one and not that one.
4. Ask the AI to pressure-test the plan, or to argue for a moment you discarded, or to attack her words the way that room would. Do not accept the first reading.
5. Turn what you concluded into the advice you would give her.

## What to hand over

Only the recommendation. The reasoning stays in the conversation with the AI.

**1. The 5% plan** — up to 250 words.

Which situations she commits to, and which she is letting go. For each one she commits to: who it is for, what perception it has to move, and the green X she is trying to leave behind. Name what she gives up; a plan that takes everything is not a plan.

**2. The words** — up to 200 words.

For the single highest-value moment in your plan, what she actually says. Not a description of her approach — the words, as she would say them out loud, in that room. If the moment is a question, write the question.

**3. The little voice** — two or three sentences.

The excuse most likely to stop her on the day, in her own words, and what she does instead when it arrives.

The evaluator sees your conversation with the AI as well as the recommendation.
